Concept

The sky is the original shared surface: the same stars for everyone, everywhere. Constellations of the Present rebuilds that surface for the network age — a collective sky that exists only in the living present, built from moments instead of profiles.

Each visitor's presence is sensed entirely on their own device. The camera reads expression, attention, and engagement in real time; the reading is quantized and immediately reduced to a non-reversible hash — a "moment-hash" — seeded with a random nonce. Only this anonymous code leaves the device, where it places a star in a constellation shared by everyone looking up at the same moment.

Stars flicker with the uncertainty of the moment that created them, grow while that moment is held, and fade when the person looks away. Connection lines form only between people present in the same instant — a web of the now.

A Sky Without Capture

Nothing raw is ever transmitted, stored, or logged in any form that could identify a person. The microphone — strictly opt-in — feeds only a live audio level into an ambient drone; it never records, never transmits a signal, and never leaves the browser. Face tracking runs entirely on-device and is discarded frame by frame.

The artwork is a working proof of its own thesis: that a collective can be built without capturing anyone.

Technical

Face geometry is analyzed locally to derive an expression bucket and engagement estimate. These quantized values, together with a random nonce, are hashed into an anonymous seed that determines the star's position and identity. No audio or video data ever leaves the device.

Artist

Andrea Cacioppo is an Italian designer and creative developer based in Milan, currently studying at Politecnico di Milano. His practice sits at the intersection of interaction design, creative code, and privacy-by-design, exploring how networked systems can create intimacy and collectivity without surveillance. His works are meant to be experienced live, in the present tense, leaving nothing behind.
